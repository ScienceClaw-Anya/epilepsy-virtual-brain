1. Executive summary

The full texts make the project more feasible but less novel than round 1 suggested. Most of the machinery the plan needs is already published with parameters you can copy. That includes the 2D-vs-6D model mismatch, simulated resection, minimal-cut interventions and a metric for "normal function". The open ground is a head-to-head benchmark of localization and intervention strategies on a shared ground truth, with per-patient adjudication. Nobody in the 1,006 screened papers has done that on VEC. Only 7 papers cite VEC so far, and none benchmarks methods on it.

Five findings that change what you build today:

  • Mismatch is the field's standard practice, not a hack. The VEP pipeline itself inverts with a 2D Epileptor and a simplified gain matrix (Wang 2023). VEC data come from the 6D model (Dollomaja 2025). Running agents on 2D against VEC's 6D data copies the published setup, and Lemaréchal 2024 shows what to expect: "correct but degraded" accuracy, driven mostly by the simplified forward model.
  • Therapeutic stimulation has no precedent in the Epileptor family as the plan describes it. In VEC and Wang 2025, stimulation accumulates toward a seizure threshold: it triggers seizures. Suppression has been shown only with closed-loop linear feedback in subject-specific Epileptor networks (Moosavi 2025), where EZ-only stimulation is limited and detection latency causes a phase transition. Alternatively, use a physiological neural mass model, as Lopez-Sola 2025 argue Epileptor is too abstract for stimulation or drug effects. "Inject periodic input and watch synchrony drop" should be replaced by one of these.
  • V2's "intervention + simulated verification" is well trodden. Wang 2023 ran virtual surgeries in VEP. An 2019 defined a suppression-ratio metric and a stimulation-based safety test. Olmi 2019 showed that cutting a minimal set of links stops propagation with far fewer cuts than removing the whole EZ. Lopez-Sola 2025 simulate surgery, stimulation and three drugs in one framework. Use these as the agents' tools and cite them.
  • The Hub theory has a weak prior, so it is a good stress test. In 91 surgical patients, hubs sat in the resected area for some and in its neighbourhood for others, and this did not track outcome (Di Gaetano 2025). In An 2019, early target choices tracked node strength (r = 0.78), but final targets after safety feedback did not (r = 0.31).
  • Score like the field scores. VEP's benchmark against clinicians on 53 patients is precision 0.613, recall 0.478, AUC 0.761, average precision 0.443. EZ regions are a small minority of 162, so report AUROC and AUPRC (Hrtonova 2025). Use train/validation/test splits and also score regions that should be preserved (Bernabei 2023).

Two corrections to round 1. Bernabei 2023 states the HUP/fragility releases include electrode coordinates and resection targets across ds003029, ds003876 (interictal) and ds004100. Round 1 found no electrode files in ds003029's git tree, so treat ds003029's coordinates as "possibly in sourcedata" and keep ds004100 as the V4 dataset. Also, the VEC randomized-cohort comparison (p<0.0001) was run on 5 of 30 patients, not the whole cohort.

4. The model and the cohort T2

Primary entries (full text)

Dollomaja et al. 2025 — VEC: what the files and parameters actually are
T2HighOpen access
Dollomaja B, et al. "Virtual epilepsy patient cohort: Generation and evaluation." PLOS Computational Biology (2025). DOI 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1012911. View paper ↗
Conclusion
VEC's ground truth is an x0 map with explicit regime boundaries, simulated with the 6D Epileptor at 0.5 ms Euler steps. Stimulated seizures come from a slow "tissue change" variable crossing a threshold.
Analysis
Score agents on recovering which regions sit in the seizure regime (x0 > −2.062), not the exact x0. The stimulated-seizure model is diagnostic by construction, so stimulation in it can only make seizures more likely. The headline validation against a randomized cohort used 5 patients, so don't present it as a 30-patient result.
Supporting evidence
For x0 values below –2.062 the model settles into a fixed point in the down-state which corresponds to an interictal state.
In this paper, this set of differential equations is solved using an Euler integration scheme with a step size of 0.5 ms.
We followed this procedure for 5 patients of our cohort, resulting in 15 simulated seizures for each EZ hypothesis.
Wang HE et al. 2023 — VEP inverts with a 2D model; full benchmark numbers
T2HighMIT full text
Wang HE, et al. "Delineating epileptogenic networks using brain imaging data and personalized modeling in drug-resistant epilepsy." Science Translational Medicine 15 (2023). DOI 10.1126/scitranslmed.abp8982. View paper ↗
Conclusion
On 53 patients, VEP vs clinicians gave precision 0.613, recall 0.478, AUC 0.761, average precision 0.443, mean distance 5.67 mm. Inversion used a 2D Epileptor with a distance-only gain matrix.
Analysis
Gives you a real benchmark row for V1 and legitimizes the 2D agent model. The paper also runs virtual surgeries: in one seizure-free patient, removing the clinical EZ alone did not stop simulated seizures, while the larger real resection was consistent with seizure freedom. That is V2, already published.
Supporting evidence
Here, the VEP inversion module was based on the NMM, with a lower dimensional model (2D Epileptor), which captures the envelope function, and a low-resolution gain matrix, which considers only the distance between the nodes and sensors (Fig. 2C).
We also introduced two threshold-free measures: the average precision score (with mean of 0.443) and the area under receiver operating characteristic curve (with mean of 0.761).
Lemaréchal et al. 2024 — a mismatch benchmark: what degrades and why
T2HighOpen access
Lemaréchal, Triebkorn, Vattikonda, et al. "Effects of the spatial resolution of the Virtual Epileptic Patient on the identification of epileptogenic networks." Imaging Neuroscience (2024). DOI 10.1162/imag_a_00153. View paper ↗
Conclusion
Synthetic SEEG from an 81,942-node neural field model, inverted with the 162-node NMM, still finds the EZN, but with worse accuracy. The simplified forward model is the main cause.
Analysis
The closest published analogue to your V1 and a template for the adversary agent: they vary EZ/PZ spatial configurations deliberately. Use VEC's own gain matrix for the agents, or the forward model alone will dominate your error.
Supporting evidence
Overall, we observed correct but degraded performance when using an NMM to estimate the EZN from data simulated with an NFM, which was significant for the simplest spatial configurations.
We quantified the reduced performance and demonstrated that the oversimplification of the forward problem is its principal cause.
Proix et al. 2014 — the 2D reduction and what stimulation does in it
T2MediumMIT full text
Proix T, et al. "Permittivity coupling across brain regions determines seizure recruitment in partial epilepsy." J Neurosci 34(45) (2014). DOI 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1570-14.2014. View paper ↗
Conclusion
The 2D model comes from averaging over fast oscillations and is checked against the full system. In coupled Epileptors, stimulation can trigger a seizure, and whether it recruits a neighbour depends on that neighbour's slow state.
Analysis
Fine for recruitment and spread metrics. It cannot carry fast-activity features like EI, and in this model family stimulation is a trigger, not a brake. The equations on the publisher page are images; take the 2D form from TVB's Epileptor2D and re-derive the critical x0.
Supporting evidence
Numerical analyses of the full system demonstrate that the effect of the second neuronal ensemble is negligible under this approximation, hence we neglect it in the analytical discussion also for simplicity, but verify all results against the full system numerically.
The ability of a seizure triggered by stimulation to recruit a second Epileptor thus depends on the state of this second Epileptor.
Proix et al. 2017 — patient vs control vs shuffled connectomes
T2HighOpen access
Proix T, Bartolomei F, Guye M, Jirsa VK. "Individual brain structure and modelling predict seizure propagation." Brain 140 (2017). DOI 10.1093/brain/awx004. View paper ↗
Conclusion
In 15 patients, individual connectomes beat degree-preserving shuffles (P < 0.01) but not healthy-control connectomes (higher in 9 of 15, n.s. at group level).
Analysis
Two ready-made controls. A shuffled connectome is the adversary's cheapest attack and should break Hub and Bottleneck. A normative connectome is a fair stand-in when you move to HUP, which has no DTI.
Supporting evidence
Scores obtained for the individualized connectivity matrices were higher than control subject connectivities for nine patients (Fig. 6 and Supplementary Fig. 3A), but the group level comparison did not show significant differences of the medians (Mann-Whitney U-test, Supplementary Fig. 2, control boxplot).
Wang H et al. 2025 — stimulation as a diagnostic, confirmed in methods
T2MediumOpen access
Wang H, et al. "Virtual brain twins for stimulation in epilepsy." Nature Computational Science (2025). DOI 10.1038/s43588-025-00841-6. View paper ↗
Conclusion
Both SEEG and temporal-interference stimulation are modelled as building up the slow variable m until a seizure starts. Validation is on synthetic data.
Analysis
Confirms the round-1 correction: cite it for the Onset agent's use of stimulated seizures, not for therapy.
Supporting evidence
Both types of stimulation generated an accumulation in the slow state variable m of the Epileptor-stimulation model (equation (1)), which represents stimulation-induced tissue changes.

5. Interventions: what is already published T3

Primary entries (full text)

An et al. 2019 — target zones, suppression ratio, safety test
T3HighOpen access
An S, Bartolomei F, Guye M, Jirsa V. "Optimization of surgical intervention outside the epileptogenic zone in the Virtual Epileptic Patient (VEP)." PLOS Comput Biol 15 (2019). DOI 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1007051. View paper ↗
Conclusion
In 7 patients, modularity analysis picked nodes or tracts between modules. Cutting tracts disturbed normal-function simulations less than removing nodes, and node strength predicted early targets (r = 0.78) but not final ones (r = 0.31).
Analysis
Gives you the Bottleneck agent's algorithm, the efficacy metric (SR) and a safety metric. Add an "edge-cut" intervention: it is cheaper by this paper's measure and lets the collective propose something other than resection.
Supporting evidence
The results also show that disconnecting the fiber tracts corresponding to the target edges has less impact on normal brain function than resecting the brain regions (corresponding to the target nodes).
Meanwhile, in deriving the TZs, the frequency of the target nodes initially acquired is positively correlated with the node strength (the sum of weights of links connected with other nodes), i.e., the nodes having high strength are frequently derived as TZs (correlation coefficient: 0.7842).
Olmi et al. 2019 — minimal link lesioning beats whole-EZ removal
T3HighOpen access
Olmi S, Petkoski S, Guye M, Bartolomei F, Jirsa V. "Controlling seizure propagation in large-scale brain networks." PLOS Comput Biol 15 (2019). DOI 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1006805. View paper ↗
Conclusion
In 15 personalized models, links chosen by linear stability analysis stopped propagation with fewer cuts than removing all EZ out-links, random cuts, or cutting the strongest links.
Analysis
The analytic Control-theory agent, plus four ready-made baselines for V2. Report "cuts needed to stop spread" next to localization accuracy.
Supporting evidence
Targeted lesioning depicts the minimal number of links that are sufficient to be removed (pink) in order to stop the seizure, versus the total number of outgoing links from the EZs (blue) that are removed during the resection of an entire EZ.
Moosavi et al. 2025 — closed-loop control of seizure spread in Epileptor networks
T3HighOpen access
Moosavi, Feldman, Truccolo, et al. "Controllability of nonlinear epileptic-seizure spreading dynamics in large-scale subject-specific brain networks." Scientific Reports (2025). DOI 10.1038/s41598-025-90632-w. View paper ↗
Conclusion
Linear feedback stimulation can stop spread in tractography-based Epileptor networks, but stimulating the EZ alone is often not enough. Control latency causes a sharp phase transition, and extra stabilizing nodes are needed.
Analysis
The only full-text precedent for therapeutic stimulation in your model family. Rebuild the stimulation arm as closed-loop feedback with a latency parameter, and let the Control-theory agent choose actuator nodes. It also contrasts with Gramian/minimum-energy measures, the kind of controllability the plan proposes.
Supporting evidence
Notably, control onset latency is a critical parameter leading to a phase transition in spread controllability.
Consequently, the efficacy of EZ-only actuation is limited depending on network excitability, coupling strength, and practical latencies for detection and actuation.
Lopez-Sola et al. 2025 — surgery, stimulation and drugs in one personalized model
T3HighMIT full text
Lopez-Sola E, Mercadal B, Lleal-Custey È, et al. "Personalized whole-brain models of seizure propagation." J Neural Eng (2025). DOI 10.1088/1741-2552/ae08e9. View paper ↗
Conclusion
Physiological neural mass models (Wendling-class, with a separate model for EZ nodes) fitted to SEEG functional connectivity simulate resection, stimulation and three antiseizure drugs, and show patient-specific responses.
Analysis
If you want the "treatment" story beyond resection (stimulation, drugs), this is the model class to use. It also says why Epileptor is the wrong tool for that.
Supporting evidence
However, their abstraction from the underlying physiology limits their use in studies requiring neurobiologically-based interpretations, such as the simulation of pharmacological effects or electrical stimulation.
Goodfellow et al. 2016 — the V4 overlap analysis, done on 16 patients
T3MediumOpen access
Goodfellow M, et al. "Estimation of brain network ictogenicity predicts outcome from epilepsy surgery." Scientific Reports 6 (2016). DOI 10.1038/srep29215. View paper ↗
Conclusion
In 16 patients, overlap between model-optimal and actual resection was higher for Engel I than Engel IV patients (p < 0.05).
Analysis
Copy this statistic for V4 on HUP. With 36 good and 22 poor outcomes you have more power than this paper did.
Supporting evidence
Pairwise comparisons revealed that the overlap was higher for Engel class I responders than Engel class IV responders (Wilcoxon rank-sum test, p < 0.05).
Kini et al. 2019 · Lopes et al. 2017 — cohort sizes behind the network-surgery claims
T3MediumMIT full text
Kini LG, Bernabei JM, et al. Brain 142 (2019), DOI 10.1093/brain/awz303 · Lopes MA, et al. PLOS Comput Biol 13 (2017), DOI 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1005637. View Kini ↗
Conclusion
Kini's AUC 0.89 comes from 28 patients (HUP and Mayo). Lopes' rich-club result comes from 16 patients (6 Engel I, 5 II, 5 IV).
Analysis
These are small cohorts. Quote them with n attached so judges don't think the field's numbers are settled.
Supporting evidence
validating a brain network model on a retrospective cohort of 28 patients with drug-resistant epilepsy implanted with intracranial electrodes prior to surgical resection
Data were collected from 16 patients (11 female, mean age 31, and median post-surgical follow up 3 years) who underwent pre-surgical monitoring at Inselspital Bern [42,62].

6. Onset and hub agents T1

Primary entries (full text)

Bartolomei et al. 2008 — the Epileptogenicity Index (missed classic)
T1HighMIT full text
Bartolomei F, Chauvel P, Wendling F. "Epileptogenicity of brain structures in human temporal lobe epilepsy: a quantified study from intracerebral EEG." Brain 131 (2008). DOI 10.1093/brain/awn111. View paper ↗
Conclusion
EI combines how strongly a region shifts to fast activity with how early it does so. Introduced on 17 MTLE patients, it underlies the clinical EZ labels used in VEP work (EI ≥ 0.4).
Analysis
The citation analysis flagged this paper: seed papers keep citing it, and round 1 left it out. It is the natural Onset-agent feature and needs no Epileptor inversion, which reduces circularity. It needs fast activity, so compute it on VEC's 6D SEEG.
Supporting evidence
This measure, called ‘Epileptogenicity Index’ (EI), is based on both spectral (appearance of fast oscillations replacing the background activity) and temporal (delay of appearance with respect to seizure onset) properties of intracerebral EEG signals.
An EI ≥ .4 was set as a threshold to define a region as the EZN.
Caveat
The second quote is from Makhalova et al. 2022 (Epilepsia, DOI 10.1111/epi.17310), which applies EI to define clinical EZ labels.
Li et al. 2021 · Grinenko et al. 2018 — data-driven onset markers
T1MediumMIT full text
Li A, et al. Nature Neuroscience 24 (2021), DOI 10.1038/s41593-021-00901-w · Grinenko, Li, Mosher, et al. "A fingerprint of the epileptogenic zone in human epilepsies." Brain 141 (2018; online 2017), DOI 10.1093/brain/awx306. View Grinenko ↗
Conclusion
Fragility was validated on 91 patients (44 successes, 47 failures). Grinenko's time-frequency fingerprint (fast activity, flattening, slow shift) addresses the hard part: telling the EZ from early propagation.
Analysis
Give the Onset agent two or three interchangeable markers (EI, fragility, fingerprint) so its own internal disagreement is visible. That matches the plan's "genuine disagreement" goal without inventing theories.
Supporting evidence
In the study population, all patients with DRE underwent invasive iEEG monitoring followed by surgical resection or laser ablation of the SOZ (44 success and 47 failure outcomes).
Differentiating the primary epileptogenic zone from regions of propagation is difficult using fast activity signal analysis.
Di Gaetano et al. 2025 — where the hubs are, and whether it matters
T1HighOpen access
Di Gaetano, Santos, Battiston, et al. "Neighbourhood topology unveils pathological hubs in the brain networks of epilepsy-surgery patients." Brain Communications (2025). DOI 10.1093/braincomms/fcaf431. View paper ↗
Conclusion
In MEG networks from 91 surgical patients, hubs sat either in the resected area or around it, varying by patient, and the pattern did not correlate with outcome.
Analysis
Use it as the Hub agent's honest prior, and have that agent consider the RA's neighbourhood, not only the top node.
Supporting evidence
Our analyses reveal that both the RA and its neighbourhood present large hub status, but with significant variability across patients.
Moreover, this variability does not correlate with surgical outcome.

7. Real data and scoring T5

Primary entry (full text)

Bernabei et al. 2023 — study-design rules and the 122-patient release
T5HighMIT full text
Bernabei JM, et al. "Quantitative approaches to guide epilepsy surgery from intracranial EEG." Brain 146 (2023). DOI 10.1093/brain/awad007. View paper ↗
Conclusion
The authors release 122 patients across ds003029, ds003876 and ds004100. They recommend train/validation/test splits, explicitly identifying tissue to preserve, and guarding against spurious covariates.
Analysis
Three rules to adopt in V3/V4: never tune on the patients you report, score "preserved" regions (your normal-rhythm metric), and check that no agent leans on electrode count. ds003876 (interictal) is new to the plan.
Supporting evidence
We propose that methods should be developed on a training cohort, optimized on a validation cohort, and performance quantified on a held-out test cohort to prevent overfitting to small datasets.
The data are organized in the standardized BIDS format on OpenNeuro, in projects ds003029, ds003876, and ds004100.
Caveat
The paper says each dataset includes standardized electrode coordinates and surgical targets. Round 1 found no electrodes.tsv in ds003029's public tree, so check before relying on it.

Abstract-level additions

  • Hrtonova et al. 2025, "Metrics for evaluation of automatic epileptogenic zone localization in intracranial electrophysiology", Clin Neurophysiol, DOI 10.1016/j.clinph.2024.11.007. 139 patients, two centres: use AUROC and AUPRC together and report class imbalance. (Full text blocked by a site check.)
  • Penas et al. 2024, "Parameter estimation in a whole-brain network model of epilepsy: comparison of parallel global optimization solvers", PLOS Comput Biol, DOI 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1011642. A benchmark of fitting methods for VEP models, so a ready "single-method" baseline family.
  • Saggio & Jirsa 2024, "Bifurcations and bursting in the Epileptor", PLOS Comput Biol, DOI 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1011903. Shows how to change Epileptor parameters to produce other seizure classes. Useful for the adversary to generate onset types VEC lacks.

8. LLMs and EZ localization T6

Primary entry (full text)

Sun et al. 2025 — zero-shot LLMs localize the EZ from clinical text
T6HighMIT full text
Sun, Ge, Wang, et al. "Can large language models aid presurgical epileptogenic zone localization? A multisource text analysis performance study." International Journal of Surgery (2025). DOI 10.1097/JS9.0000000000004435. View paper ↗
Conclusion
GPT-4.1, Claude 3.7 Sonnet and DeepSeek-R1 (temperature 0, no fine-tuning) lateralized the EZ at 98.3–100% and reached a median lobar score of 70 in 154 seizure-free patients.
Analysis
Real evidence that LLMs can reason about EZ localization, but from reports, at lobe level, and only in seizure-free patients (a selection bias). In your design the LLMs should orchestrate tools and argue over their outputs. They should not localize from raw signals. Say this explicitly.
Supporting evidence
All models achieved 98.3% accuracy in Cohort 1 and 100% in Cohort 2 for laterality.
In lobar localization, GPT-4.1 and Claude 3.7 Sonnet achieved a median score of 70, significantly higher than Deepseek-R1’s 60 (P < 0.001) in Cohort 1.

Abstract-level addition

  • Yang et al. 2026, "Epilepsy domain adapted large language models enhance epileptogenic zone prediction from seizure semiology", Communications Medicine, DOI 10.1038/s43856-026-01883-1. A fine-tuned model trained on 1,372 semiology–EZ pairs, 100 held out for evaluation.

9. Revised experiment plan

  1. 1
    Agents get published tools, not invented ones. Onset uses EI, fragility or an Epileptor fit. Hub uses strength and neighbourhood centrality. Bottleneck uses An 2019 modularity or Olmi 2019 link cuts. Control uses Moosavi 2025 feedback nodes. Greedy runs simulated search.
  2. 2
    Generator ≠ agent model. VEC 6D data, agents simulate in 2D, VEC's own gain matrix. Expect the degradation Lemaréchal 2024 reports.
  3. 3
    V1 scoring. Score against VEP and clinical hypotheses separately. Report precision/recall/F0.5 at 0.5, plus AUROC and AUPRC, with VEP's 0.613 / 0.478 / 0.761 as a reference row.
  4. 4
    V2 scoring. Suppression ratio, number of nodes or links removed, and the An 2019 safety score. Baselines: whole-EZ removal, random cuts, strongest links (Olmi 2019).
  5. 5
    Stimulation arm. Closed-loop feedback with an explicit latency sweep (Moosavi 2025), or drop it and say why. Do not use open-loop periodic input in Epileptor.
  6. 6
    Adversary. Shuffled connectome, normative connectome, alternative seizure classes (Saggio & Jirsa 2024), and multifocal EZ configurations (Lemaréchal 2024).
  7. 7
    V3/V4 hygiene. Use a fixed held-out set of VEC patients for memory evaluation. On HUP ds004100, train/validation/test splits and the Goodfellow overlap-vs-Engel test.
