1. Executive summary

Bottom line: the project is feasible today as a methods benchmark, and the data are real and open. It is not yet a defensible "better treatment" claim. The Virtual Epileptic Cohort (VEC) is on EBRAINS as free access, CC BY 4.0, with 30 virtual patients on a 162-region atlas, exactly as the plan says. A real-patient check is also possible, but with a different dataset than the one in the plan.

Four statements in the plan need correcting before the pitch:

  • Wang et al. 2025 is in Nature Computational Science, not Nature, and it models stimulation-induced seizures for diagnosing the epileptogenic zone network. It is not a model of therapeutic, seizure-suppressing stimulation, so it cannot be "the basis for the stimulation model" in V2.
  • "Existing VEP work does localization" understates prior work. An et al. 2019 and Olmi et al. 2019 already used VEP-style models to propose interventions outside the EZ and test them in simulation. V2 is not what separates you from prior work. The head-to-head benchmark and per-patient adjudication are.
  • EPINOV is not "clinical validation". ClinicalTrials.gov lists NCT03643016 with status Unknown, 356 planned patients, an estimated primary completion of Dec 2024 and no posted results. Say "under evaluation in a randomized trial".
  • "Surgeons cut the wrong place about half the time" overreaches. About half of adults are seizure-free 5–10 years after surgery (52% at 5 y, 47% at 10 y), but documented failure mechanisms include disease progression as well as failure to define or resect the EZ.

Three design risks a judge can find in five minutes:

  • Circularity. VEC's "ground truth" is the Epileptor parameter set used to generate its SEEG, so an agent that inverts Epileptor, and a greedy search run in the same simulator, start with a home advantage. "The seizure stopped in simulation" is partly true by construction. Fix: score against both of VEC's EZ hypotheses (VEP-inferred and clinical), run agents in a deliberately mismatched model, and add a real-patient check.
  • "Seizure = hypersynchrony" is outdated. Desynchronization often comes before or early in seizures, and synchronization rises toward termination. A drop in the Kuramoto index is therefore not a valid efficacy metric on its own. Lead with recruitment and propagation.
  • V4 cannot run on ds003029 as written. The public release has 35 subjects but no MRI and no electrode coordinates, and resection status sits in a clinical spreadsheet rather than the channel files. The HUP iEEG dataset (OpenNeuro ds004100) fits better: 58 patients, fsaverage electrode coordinates, per-channel resect/soz labels, Engel outcomes, CC0.

On the agent collective: published head-to-heads find that multi-agent debate "does not reliably outperform" single-agent prompting strategies, and that multi-agent gains on benchmarks are "often minimal". The plan's V3 design (equal budgets, single-agent and single-theory baselines, shuffled order) is the right test. Pre-commit to reporting a null result. The good news for the premise is that published network-surgery models show the best strategy depends on network topology (Lopes 2017), which is the condition under which per-patient selection should beat any fixed method.

3. Methodological risks and concrete fixes

R1 · Circularity ("inverse crime")

VEC states that its key parameters, "which include the EZ hypothesis, serve as the ground truth for simulated brain activity". The SEEG your agents see was generated by the same model family whose parameters they are scored on. That inflates the Onset agent if it inverts Epileptor, and makes Greedy search an in-model oracle. In V2, resecting the true high-excitability nodes will stop seizures by construction.

  • Score twice: against the VEP hypothesis and the clinical hypothesis that VEC also ships. A method that wins only on the first is fitting the model, not the brain.
  • Mismatch on purpose: data come from 6D Epileptor; let agents run 2D Epileptor (or Wilson–Cowan) and perturb the connectome and gain matrix in the adversary (noise, dropped edges, a shuffled hemisphere).
  • Reframe V2: report the cost of each plan (regions removed, normal-rhythm preservation) against the greedy minimum, not "the seizure stopped".
  • Anchor on real data (V4): even a small check on HUP turns "it works in our simulator" into "it agrees with surgeons on cases that went well".

R2 · Patient-specific connectomes add less than assumed

Proix et al. 2017 found that individual models explain propagation and surgical variability "but do not reliably augment with the use of patient-specific connectivity". The Hub and Bottleneck agents lean entirely on the connectome. Add a control in which each patient gets a normative or shuffled connectome. If the agents' ranking doesn't change, the connectome is not doing the work.

R3 · The efficacy metric

Use recruitment extent and propagation as the primary outcome and synchrony as a secondary one. VEC's own validation shows the seizure-propagation metric for stimulated seizures did not separate the true cohort from a randomized one. Keep the stimulation arm exploratory and limited to the 14 patients with stimulation data.

R4 · Statistics for 30 patients

Use paired, per-patient comparisons (Wilcoxon or permutation) with bootstrap CIs. Run ≥3 LLM seeds per condition, and keep the shuffled-order control from the plan. Hold out 10 patients that no condition ever writes memory from, and report accuracy on them.

5. Virtual-brain stack and the VEC dataset T2

The modeling chain (Epileptor → VEP → VEC) is well documented and openly released. The weak links are the stimulation citation and the trial claim.

6. The five localization theories: prior evidence T3

Every agent in the plan has a published precedent, and several papers already show these methods disagree by topology. That makes the collective's premise stronger. It also means the novelty lies in the comparison and adjudication, not in any single method or in intervention-by-simulation.

7. Synchrony as the objective T4

The plan's explainer ("a seizure is the extreme form of synchrony") is the textbook view that the field has moved away from. Keep the clapping analogy for the audience, but don't build the main metric on it.

Source (abstract-level)

Jiruska et al. 2013 — synchronization and desynchronization in epilepsy
T4HighAbstract only
Jiruska P, de Curtis M, Jefferys JGR, Schevon CA, Schiff SJ, Schindler K. "Synchronization and desynchronization in epilepsy: controversies and hypotheses." J Physiol (2013). DOI 10.1113/jphysiol.2012.239590. View paper ↗
Conclusion
Seizures involve heterogeneous firing and changing synchrony. Desynchronization often appears early, and high synchrony near the end may help terminate them.
Analysis
An intervention that lowers a global Kuramoto index could prolong a seizure. Make recruitment extent and seizure duration the primary V2 outcomes, report synchrony as a secondary metric, and keep normal-rhythm preservation as the hard constraint. Note that Epileptor is not a phase oscillator, so the order parameter must be computed from Hilbert phases of simulated signals.
Supporting evidence
Desynchronization is often observed preceding seizures or during their early stages; in contrast, high levels of synchronization observed towards the end of seizures may facilitate termination.

9. Evidence for LLM agent collectives T6

Multi-agent science systems have real wins, but controlled comparisons often find little gain over a single agent given the same budget. That makes V3 a genuine experiment rather than a foregone conclusion. Present it that way.

10. Revised evidence chain and pitch lines

The chain below replaces the plan's implicit one. Each link names the source that carries it.

  1. 1
    Need. About a third of patients stay drug-resistant (Chen 2018), and about half of surgical patients are seizure-free at 5–10 years (de Tisi 2011), worse for extratemporal cases.
  2. 2
    Gap. Part of the failure is incomplete localization or resection (Najm 2013). Model-based EZ estimates agree only partly with clinicians (VEP recall 44%, Makhalova 2022).
  3. 3
    Competing methods. Onset/fragility, betweenness, rich-club, controllability and propagation-cut methods each have published support (Li 2021; Wilke 2011; Lopes 2017; Scheid 2021; An 2019), and the best one depends on network topology (Lopes 2017).
  4. 4
    Testbed. VEC gives 30 open virtual patients with known model parameters and both VEP and clinical EZ hypotheses (Dollomaja 2025). This is the first setting where all methods can be scored against the same answer.
  5. 5
    Contribution. A head-to-head benchmark plus a per-patient agent collective that adjudicates with a simulator verifier, tested at matched budget against single-agent, self-consistency and single-method baselines.
  6. 6
    Robustness. Agents run in a mismatched model (2D vs 6D) on perturbed connectomes. Results reported against both EZ hypotheses and with a normative-connectome control (Proix 2017).
  7. 7
    Reality check. On HUP ds004100 (58 patients), test whether overlap between each method's proposal and the resected tissue tracks Engel outcome, following Goodfellow 2016 and Kini 2019.

Suggested opening lines (replacing the plan's three)

  • "For about a third of people with epilepsy, drugs don't work. Surgery helps, but only about half are seizure-free five to ten years later."
  • "Researchers have proposed several computational ways to decide where to intervene, and published results show each one wins on some brains and loses on others."
  • "We put each method in its own agent, let them argue over 30 open virtual patients with known answers, and check the winners against 58 real surgical cases."

Updated answers for the Q&A table

  • "Isn't this just VEP?" VEP and An 2019 each propose one answer per patient. We are the first to score several methods head-to-head on a shared ground truth and to pick per patient, with the pick verified in a model that didn't generate the data.
  • "Is the simulation realistic?" Quote Jirsa 2023: virtual brains are promising and still being tested in trials. Our claim is relative: which method ranks best, robust to model mismatch.
  • "Does multi-agent help?" We tested it against matched-budget single-agent and self-consistency baselines (Smit 2024) and report the result either way.
